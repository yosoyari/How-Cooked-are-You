ABOUT THIS STUDY: SOCIAL MEDIA AS AN AGENT OF SECONDARY SOCIALISATION

In classical sociology, primary socialisation occurs during childhood within the family, while secondary socialisation takes place later through institutions such as schools, peer groups, and media. Today, algorithmic platforms function as pervasive agents of secondary socialisation that continuously teach, reward, and police behavioural scripts among young people.

Rather than enforcing norms through formal rules, social media relies on quantified peer validation (likes, ratios, views), algorithmic visibility, and horizontal surveillance (monitoring reposts, unliking reels, curating close-friends lists). Being "cooked" vernacularly describes the state in which an individual's language, romantic expectations, taste formation, self-worth, and political expression become deeply standardized by internet culture.
